## §4 (add) — The problem is measured, national, and worsening

Marg AI exists to fight **decision fatigue** — the mental exhaustion and paralysis that sets in
when people face too many choices amid rapid change. This is not a niche concern; it is a
measured, population-scale problem in India.

**Scale of the problem** (survey of Indian adults, reported by *The Financial Express*, 2024):

| Finding | Figure |
|---|---|
| Struggle to plan for the future amid rapid change | **69%** |
| Experience decision paralysis — overwhelmed, unable to act | **33% (1 in 3)** |
| Feel ill-equipped to manage decisions effectively | **62%** |
| Regret missed opportunities from delaying or avoiding decisions | **57%** |

**Why it is worse in India:**
- **Rapid societal change** — urbanization, digital platforms and shifting family structures add
  new layers of decisions.
- **Cultural expectations** — high pressure to "manage everything smoothly," especially for
  caregivers and homemakers.
- **Information overload** — from endless product choices to hundreds of streaming and scheme
  options, modern life magnifies the load.

**Psychological impact:** cognitive overload depletes mental resources (decision quality falls by
evening); constant decision-making elevates cortisol (fatigue, irritability); depleted people
either avoid decisions or make impulsive ones.

**Everyday context:** micro-decisions at work and home (what to cook, which task to prioritize,
how to respond to family/neighbours), work-life overlap, and joint-family dynamics multiply small
but draining choices — concentrated in urban households.

**Emerging coping strategies** (which Marg operationalizes): simplification of choices, shared
responsibility, mindful pauses, and — most relevant to Marg — **professional guidance to reduce
uncertainty at life's big crossroads.**

**Product implication.** These numbers are the demand case for Marg. Marg does not add options; it
*reduces cognitive load* at the decisions that matter most — laying out the real field, never
issuing a verdict, and surfacing what people would otherwise miss (the "57% regret" cohort).

*Sources: The Financial Express (decision-paralysis survey); livolite.in; Happiest Health.*

## §14 (add) — India's own institutions are tackling decision fatigue

Decision fatigue is recognized inside government, where several bodies are actively reducing
cognitive load — for citizens and public servants alike. Marg is the **citizen-facing complement**
to this: the same clarity, applied to personal life choices.

| Ministry / Department | Initiative | How it reduces decision fatigue |
|---|---|---|
| **Dept. of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances (DARPG)** | e-Office, delayering of decision levels, Swachhata campaign | Fewer approval levels and less file movement → fewer bottlenecks and clearer citizen services |
| **Ministry of Communications — Dept. of Telecommunications (via NICF / NCA-Finance)** | Webinars on *Strengthening Cognitive Wellbeing for Effective Decision-Making* | Trains public servants to manage cognitive overload, attention and clarity under stress |
| **Dept. of Personnel & Training (DoPT)** | Capacity-building, stress-management workshops | Better decision-making skills; reduced burnout among civil servants |
| **NITI Aayog** | Policy / scheme simplification, digital-governance push | Fewer, clearer choices in citizen-facing schemes |
| **Ministry of Electronics & IT (MeitY)** | Digital India, single-window portals | Unified access → fewer confusing decision points across platforms |

**Positioning.** Government efforts simplify *processes and services*. Marg addresses the
*life-stage decisions themselves* — education, career, later-life direction — connecting citizens
to the very schemes (NEP, Skill India, scholarships) these bodies run, while lowering the mental
load of choosing. This directly advances the public mission and the national goal of a citizenry
that decides with confidence.

*Sources: DARPG (darpg.gov.in); National Institute of Communication Finance (nicf.gov.in) —
"Strengthening Cognitive Wellbeing for Effective Decision-Making".*
